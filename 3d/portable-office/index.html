The deploy

Zero to office in five moves.

Lay it flat, open the lid, and the rest is mostly gas-strut assisted: the lift raises the screens, the wings fan out, the boom rises, the keyboard slides forward. The whole sequence fits a 110-second budget. Drag the slider, tap a step, or press play.

How it fits

Four mechanisms. One carry‑on.

Three 14″ screens side by side are 93 cm wide; the case is 55. Everything below exists to close that gap — and to put the screens, camera and keys where your body expects them.

01 · The triptych fold

Three screens that fold into one.

The center panel is fixed. Two wing panels hinge on vertical axes and fold flat, face to face, over it for travel — a stack about 31 × 19.5 × 2.4 cm. Deployed, each wing swings out ~35° into a curved, immersive array.

  • 3× 14″ 16:10 thin-film OLED
  • 2.4 cm folded stack
  • 3× 2560×1600 target

02 · The lift

Raise and turn, in one motion.

The screens travel lying flat. A rear four-bar linkage, gas-strut assisted, raises them ~12 cm and rotates them flat-to-vertical, putting the center of the screen near seated eye level on a standard 74 cm desk. A straight telescoping mast could only do half of that.

  • +12 cm rise
  • Flat → vertical
  • Eye level on a 74 cm desk

03 · Pro AV

Look into the lens, not down at it.

A cardioid condenser mic rides a telescoping boom on the display bridge and angles toward your mouth. A 4K camera sits with it at eye level. Over-ear studio headphones stow in a molded cradle in the lid on pogo-pin charging; stereo speakers live in the front edge of the base.

  • Cardioid condenser mic
  • 4K camera at eye level
  • Studio headphones on board

04 · Input

A real keyboard, to the millimetre.

A low-profile mechanical TKL keyboard (36 cm) and a 16 × 11 cm glass haptic trackpad sit inline: 52 cm, inside a 53 cm interior. The keyboard rides a tray that slides ~6 cm toward you for wrist comfort.

  • 36 cm mechanical TKL
  • 16 × 11 cm haptic glass
  • 52 of 53 cm used

The compute bay

Swap computers by swapping a printed cradle.

The left third of the base is a standardized 200 × 200 × 100 mm bay with a fixed blind-mate connector block. Your machine rides in a device-specific 3D-printed cradle that drops in on a quarter-turn latch and routes its ports to that block. The case side never changes.

  • Mac mini cradle197 × 197 × 36 mmA printed riser lifts the mini flush with the bay lip; airflow lines up with its intake and exhaust.
  • Mac Studio cradle197 × 197 × 95 mmA minimal frame. The bay is sized for the tallest target device and everything shorter.
  • Mini-PC cradleParametric STLPrint to any machine’s size and port map — NUC, Framework Desktop, Beelink and more.

Your next computer is a reprint, not a new case — a cradle the plan prices at $15–35, pre-fit or printed from an open STL family.
